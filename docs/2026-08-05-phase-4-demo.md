# Phase 4 demo — Certifications & Shipping (2026-08-05)

A walkthrough for the owner: what shipped across the 21 tasks of Phase 4 (the 20 planned plus
14b), the five new E2E flows with their screenshots, how to watch it live, what changed for daily
use, and the deviations from your printed samples that you'll notice — each one a recorded
decision, not an accident.

## What Phase 4 delivered

Your orders now **certify and ship**. A part (or its customer, or the plant default) decides
whether work needs a certification and at what scope — by order, by load, or by shipment — and the
order **freezes that answer at save**, overridable while keying it. The cert itself opens
pre-loaded with one requirement per part-line inspection (min/max, scale, sample qty and location
**copied and frozen** from the part, so editing the part next month never rewrites a cert already
being filled in), takes as many readings as quality actually made, computes pass/fail per reading
with an explicit, audited override — and prints as the shop's real certification layout, built from
your sample: the standing statement, the P.O.-and-scale line, and a **bare grid of reading
values**. Min/max, scale, pass/fail and override marks stay on screen and never reach paper.

Shipping is a document, the way the shop already thinks about it: a **packing list** with one
global number, covering one or several of a customer's orders (five orders on a truck is five
tickets and one BOL — there is no separate "multi-order shipper" anything), each order carrying its
own never-reused shipment sequence — the `-3` in `72036-3`. Ship-now quantities prefill to
**ordered minus already-shipped**; over-shipping warns and never blocks ("we just ship"); the
**line complete** checkbox — a human decision, never arithmetic — is what flips an order to
Partially Shipped or Shipped on the board. A customer on **credit hold cannot be shipped** unless
the person saving holds the `override_credit_hold` action and types a reason, which lands in the
audit history and prints nowhere. Voiding a shipment needs a reason, locks every control, restores
the orders' statuses, keeps every stored PDF reprintable forever, and never frees a number or a
sequence. Every ticket, BOL and certification is archived byte-for-byte at print, exactly like the
traveler.

All of it is covered by **1357 integration tests** (from 1010 at branch start) and the
fifteen-flow Playwright harness this task adds five flows to (spec §13). Spec:
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-04-phase-4-certs-shipping-design.md`. Plan:
`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-08-04-phase-4-certs-shipping.md`. Your four production samples in
`docs/samples/` were the layout contract throughout.

## Seed state

Nothing beyond the standard seed: `npm run db:seed`. The E2E run below creates its own throwaway
fixtures in the dev database — on top of the Phase 2C/3 fixture customers, Task 20 adds a plain
shipping customer (`E2ESHIPCUST`) with two ordinary parts and one **cert-required** part carrying
two real inspection requirements (Hardness 40–50 HRC, Case Depth 0.02–0.045), a customer on
**credit hold on purpose** (`E2EHOLDCUST`), an `E2E Tote` container type, and a third fixture user
— a **shipping clerk** who can key orders and shipments but does *not* hold
`override_credit_hold`, because the credit-hold flow's first half is exactly that person hitting
the gate. Everything is torn back out afterward — shipments, certs, stored PDFs and audit rows
included — on success, on a thrown error, or on Ctrl-C mid-run.

## The five new E2E flows

Run with `npm run test:e2e` from `erp/` — this now runs **fifteen** flows in sequence (the ten
from 2C-3/Phase 3, unchanged, plus these five). Screenshots and a `video.webm` per flow land in
`erp/e2e-artifacts/<flow>/` (gitignored — reviewed locally, not committed). Unlike Phase 3's four
order flows, each Phase 4 flow creates its **own** orders through the real entry page, so none of
them depends on another's leftovers.

### 11. `ship-partial-then-complete` — the ship ledger, end to end
Keys a two-line order (100 of part A, 40 of part B), then ships from `/shipping/new`: the grid
prefills every line to its full remainder, the flow ships 60 of line 1 and nothing of line 2,
completing nothing — and the board now shows **Partially shipped** next to the traffic light. A
second shipment then proves the ledger: its prefill arrives already at **40** (ordered 100 minus
the 60 shipped), the flow deliberately ships **50** anyway and ticks both **Complete** boxes — and
the save stops on the amber **"Packing List N saved."** panel listing *"shipping 50 / 400 lbs
exceeds the remaining 40 / 400 lbs on this line"*: over-shipping warned, visibly, and still saved
(§5.7 — warns, never blocks). The board flips to **Shipped**, because both lines are complete —
the over-shipped quantity has no say in it (status is the human checkbox, never arithmetic).

This flow also carries a deliberate regression pin from Task 17: on the order hub it saves a
container with a **Cust Cont Id** (`BIN-0007`), then saves again touching *only* the count, and
after a full page reload asserts the stored id is still there — the exact save shape that used to
silently blank the column.

**Demonstrates:** remainder prefill and shipped-to-date (spec §5.1), over-ship
warns-never-blocks (§5.7) rendered in a real browser, status derivation from line-complete alone
(§5.2), and the Cust Cont Id round-trip fix (§3.22 / Task 17).

### 12. `multi-order-shipment` — two orders, one truck: tickets, BOL, and the shared archive
Keys two orders (one for the cert-required part — its by-order cert is created by the order save
itself) and puts both on one shipment from `/shipping/new`: one panel per order, each labeled with
its own sequence (`N-1`). Edits a line through the shipment page's grid (ship 8 of 10 — the saved
value round-trips through the server). **Print all tickets** (the "Also print certifications" box
comes pre-ticked) archives ONE whole-set document — the flow fetches the stored PDF's own bytes
and asserts it carries **exactly two sheets**, one ticket per order, never a merged "MOS" sheet
(§3.20) — and reports *"1 certification archived"* for the covered order's cert. **Print BOL**
lazily allocates the BOL number (§3.19) and the page header now reads "· BOL N". Then both order
hubs are visited: **each lists the same stored documents** — the same BOL row (asserted to be the
identical archived document, not a copy), the ticket set, and the cert — because a multi-order
shipment's paper belongs on every order it covers (§8's union).

The tail end pins this phase's last owner ruling in a real browser: the flow **voids the cert**
(reason required), prints tickets again with the cert box still ticked — and the tickets print
anyway while an amber warning names the order: *"requires a certification and none exists to print
— its ticket printed without one"*. The refusal-to-warning conversion (owner ruling 2026-08-05),
delivered via the `x-print-warnings` response header and rendered beside the print bar.

**Demonstrates:** emergent multi-order shipments with one ship-to (§3.12), one-ticket-per-order +
one BOL (§3.20), lazy BOL numbering (§3.19), the cross-order document union (§8), the shipment
page's grids (Task 14's browser recapture), and missing-cert warns-never-blocks at print time.

**What the printed ticket and BOL actually look like** (open `tickets.pdf` / `bol.pdf` with any
PDF viewer): the ticket is your sample's layout section for section — Sold To / Ship To blocks,

### 13. `cert-results-print` — seeded, computed, overridden, printed, archived exactly
Keys an order for the cert-required part and opens its certification straight from the hub — the
cert is already there (created at order save, §6.2) and already **seeded**: two requirement
blocks, one per part inspection, showing the frozen scale/min/max/sample-qty/location with the
note that part changes never rewrite them. The flow types readings and watches the verdicts
compute **live, before any save**: 45 → Pass, 60 → Fail (the same `computePassed` the server
runs — the screen can never disagree with what gets stored). After saving, the summary reads **"1
passed · 1 failed · 0 pending"** — three explicit states; a reading nobody has entered is
*pending*, never quietly a pass. It then **overrides** the failing reading to Pass — an explicit
checkbox plus verdict choice, flagged and audited — and the summary flips to "2 passed". Printing
archives the document and stamps the first-print date; the flow fetches the stored PDF **twice**
and asserts the bytes are identical (the reprint guarantee: stored paper never changes), then
prints once more and shows the archive growing — every print action is its own stored document,
forever.

**Demonstrates:** cert creation timing (§6.2), seeding + freezing from the part (§4.1/§6.3),
live computed pass/fail with the three-state summary, the audited override, §3.21's
screen-only rule (the page itself says pass/fail never prints — see the deviations below), and
byte-exact stored documents (§12.12).

`06-override-round-trip.png`, `07-cert-printed.png`, `08-reprint-archived.png`,
`certification.pdf`, `video.webm`.

**What the printed certification actually looks like** (`certification.pdf`): your sample's

### 14. `void-shipment` — a void locks everything and frees nothing
Ships an order complete (the hub shows **Shipped**), prints its ticket, then voids the shipment —
the prompt spells out exactly what that means and requires a reason. Then the flow proves §5.6 in
the DOM, not by eyeballing: it sweeps **every input, select, textarea and button on the page** and
asserts each one is disabled or read-only — the per-control-type lock ("looks disabled" and
"cannot be edited" are different claims), with the print button's tooltip reading *"Shipment is
voided — stored prints stay available"* and the void button's *"Already voided"*. The stored
ticket is still listed and still downloads (fetched and verified as a real PDF). The order's
status **returns to Open** — the voided shipment's line-complete flag no longer counts. And a new
shipment for the same order prefills back at the **full 30** (a voided shipment contributes
nothing to the ledger) and lands as sequence **`N-2`** — the voided `-1` is never reused, exactly
as a voided order keeps its number.

**Demonstrates:** void-with-required-reason, the total control lockdown (per control type), stored
prints surviving forever, status restoration, ledger exclusion of voided shipments, and
never-reused sequence numbers (§4.2/§5.6).

### 15. `credit-hold-block-and-override` — the gate, from both sides of the permission
Runs as the fixture **shipping clerk** — a user who can key orders and shipments but does not
hold `override_credit_hold`. Keying the held customer's *order* warns and saves (credit hold never
blocks entry — Phase 3's ruling; the squeeze is at shipping). On `/shipping/new`, the moment the
held customer is picked, the red banner names them and **links to their record**; the clerk sees
*"Saving a shipment for them requires the override_credit_hold action"*, gets **no reason field**
(a control that could never lead anywhere is a dead end, not an affordance), and Save is disabled
with a tooltip naming the missing action — blocked, and told exactly why and by what. The flow
then signs in as the admin (who holds the action): the banner now carries the required reason
field, a **blank reason is refused**, a real one saves the shipment — and the flow asserts the
reason landed in the shipment's create **audit entry** (`creditHoldOverrideReason`), where it
lives forever and prints on nothing.

**Demonstrates:** the credit-hold gate (owner ruling §3.7 — this phase's headline), §5.16's
disabled-but-explained rule, the named-and-linked refusal (§5.4), the required override reason,
and reason-reaches-audit-only.

## What changed for daily use

- **Shipping** (left nav) is a searchable list of every packing list — filter by customer, ship
  date range, or search by packing-list / BOL / order number; include-voided toggle; Excel export.
  **New Shipment** builds the whole document on one screen: pick the customer (their standing
  shipping notes and any credit hold show immediately), pick a ship-to from their saved addresses,
  add their unshipped orders, and every line arrives prefilled to what's left to ship. One Save
  allocates the packing-list number, each order's sequence, and any by-shipment certs, atomically
  — an abandoned form burns nothing.
- **The shipment page** is one panel per order: lines (ordered / shipped-to-date / ship-now /
  complete), containers, and serials, plus the freight block (class, description, prepaid/collect,
  pro number, SCAC — all on your BOL sample), ticket and BOL printing, the document archive, and
  History. **Void shipment** needs a reason and locks the page forever; stored paper stays.
- **Certifications** (left nav) is the quality worklist: every cert with its order, scope,
  printed state and a three-state results summary (passed / failed / pending). The cert page
  itself is where readings are typed — verdicts compute as you type, overrides are explicit and
  flagged, freeform notes print, internal notes never do. After a cert has printed, editing its
  results needs the separate `edit_cert_results_after_print` action.
- **The order hub** gained two sections: **Certifications** (every cert for the order, voided
  ones dimmed; by-load gaps shown explicitly — "4 loads · 0 certs" with a create button per
  uncovered load — and a warning when a re-split orphans a load cert) and **Shipments** (every
  shipment that ever carried the order, with your sequence — `72036-3` — quantities and
  complete flags). The Documents section now shows tickets, BOLs and certs alongside travelers.
- **The board** now actually moves: Partially Shipped and Shipped light up from the shipper's
  line-complete checkboxes — nothing else.
- **Order edits after shipping** stay open (PO, dates, notes, containers, serials, loads,
  charges) — only three things refuse, each naming the blocking shipment: removing a shipped
  line, reducing a line below its shipped-to-date, and voiding an order with live shipments.
- **Admin → Users** gained the signature upload (PNG/JPEG/GIF/WebP): whoever prints a
  certification signs it — their image, or their typed name until they upload one.
- **Settings** gained the shipping/cert numbering seeds and two standing text blocks — the
  certification statement and the ticket liability text — editable without touching code.

## Deviations from your samples you will notice (§3.22 and friends)

All three are recorded decisions with your words behind them, not gaps:

1. **Pass/fail shows on screen and never on paper.** Your certification sample prints a bare grid
   of reading values — no min/max, no scale column, no pass/fail (§3.21). The model keeps all of
   it (quality needs it to work), the screen shows all of it, and the printed document carries
   values only. The cert page says this right under its pass/fail summary, so nobody hunts for a
   "print the verdicts" switch that doesn't exist.
2. **`cert_number_next` sits in Settings, unused — deliberately.** Your samples proved a
   certification has no number of its own: it prints the order number and shipment sequence
   (`72036-3`). The setting stays in the registry (spec §3.19 records why) so a future reader
   doesn't "fix" it by wiring it up; nothing consumes it.
3. **Cust Cont Id and Customer Job No are built but empty for this shop.** Your instruction,
   verbatim: *"While our shop does not use them, they are handy and are used frequently by other
   shops and some contracts."* They're real columns on the container grids and the order (and the
   ticket prints Cust Cont Id) — expect them to sit blank in daily use here.

Two smaller print-side calls already flagged for your ruling at the PR (with two others, listed in
the handoff): the ticket prints no "Page N of M" (the layout system can't carry page-count logic
until Phase 7's designer), and the cert signature block prints name + company but no title line —
there is no title field on users yet; a small follow-up adds it if you want one.
